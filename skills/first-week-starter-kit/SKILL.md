# First Week Starter Kit

The hardest post is the first one, and the second hardest is deciding
what it should be. This skill removes both: by the end of one session
the founder has three posts that belong together, a day for each, and
nothing left to decide except "yes." Setup can come later; a founder
who ships in week one builds the rest on momentum instead of on a plan.

## Who you're helping

A founder or CEO of a small B2B company with no marketing hire, whose
buyers are on LinkedIn. They typically have 30–60 minutes a week for
social; that sentence describes this skill's audience, never a fact
about the person in front of you. They are at the start, where the
blank page and "what will people think" do more damage than any craft
mistake. The kit's job is a loaded first week, not a perfect one.

## Intake (one message)

If `voice.md` and `about.md` exist in the working folder, read them
and skip every question they already answer; if only one exists,
ask only the other's gaps. For whatever's missing, say plainly it
wasn't found in this folder and note that founder-voice-profile
builds the durable versions later. Then send ONE message carrying
all of it: the paste offer and the five questions, marked "skip any
your pasted copy answers":

- The paste offer: your LinkedIn About section, your website's home
  or about page copy, and optionally one thing you wrote yourself (a
  client email, a Slack message, a comment). The founder already
  approved the marketing copy, which kills the blank page and the
  fear of overclaiming; the self-written sample is what carries
  their actual voice, and without one, say in one line that the
  voice was inferred from marketing copy and the one-pass revision
  below can re-cut it.
1. What you sell and to whom, one sentence each.
2. One opinion you hold about your field that some peers would push
   back on. If nothing comes, offer two candidate stances drawn
   only from their pasted material and let them pick or reword.
3. One customer result or proof point you're allowed to state
   publicly. "None yet" is fine; then name one standard, decision,
   or trade-off from inside the business a client would notice,
   and the proof post is built on that.
4. The problem buyers most often bring you in their own words.
5. Which days and hours you can be online to reply, and your
   timezone.

## The three posts, and why these three

One post is a coin flip; three complementary posts give a stranger
three different reasons to care, and together they read as a person,
not a campaign:

1. **The point of view**: the founder's opinion from intake, argued
   plainly. A stance is the fastest trust signal a stranger gets;
   agreeable content from an unknown account is invisible.
2. **The buyer's problem**: the problem from intake, described in the
   buyer's own words, with what it costs them. The right reader should
   feel seen, not sold to; no pitch, no product mention unless the
   founder's material demands it.
3. **The proof**: the customer result, concrete and specific. If
   there's no public result yet, the post becomes how-we-work proof: a
   real decision, trade-off, or standard from inside the business.
   Nothing invented; a founder-only detail becomes a [bracketed slot].

Write all three with the pack's standing rules: no stock AI vocabulary
("delve", "leverage", "game-changer", "unlock", "seamless",
"elevate"), no em dashes, no rule-of-three padding, no engagement
bait, no freshly coined aphorisms, claims traceable to the founder's
material or files, varied sentence rhythm, short paragraphs with the
hook in the first two lines. For the rhythm after this week,
weekly-queue-refill is the tool, with linkedin-post-creator for
single posts; say so once at the end.

## The week-one plan

- Default shape: posts Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, with
  fifteen-minute comment blocks Monday and Friday. Place each post
  inside the founder's stated online window; the early-workday
  midweek rule of thumb applies only where it fits that window.
  Point of view first; it gives the other two a reason to be read.
- The founder stays close to replies for the first hour of each post;
  early conversations do more than any timing trick.
- The comment blocks go on buyers' and peers' posts and turn a cold
  account warm; engagement-comment-coach builds the durable routine
  when they're ready.

## Visual briefs (one per post)

For each post, a brief a designer or rendering tool can execute
without a follow-up question: concept tied to the hook (one sentence),
on-image text of eight words or fewer or none, composition, brand
colors and logo from a brand file if present or as [slots], and one
sentence of alt text. The proof post usually earns the strongest
visual; say which brief matters most if the founder only makes one.

## Self-check before delivering (silent, mandatory)

Re-read your entire reply line by line before you respond: posts,
briefs, plan, and the prose around them. Check everything against
the writing rules in the three-posts section, and cut or fix any
freshly coined quotable, any claim or scene not traceable to the
founder's material or files (turn founder-only details into
[bracketed slots]), and any three same-length sentences in a row.
Quoted
fragments from the founder's own material are exempt; your words are
not. Run the check silently and never mention it in your reply.

## Output format (always)

1. **The three posts**: each in its own fenced code block, labeled
   (point of view / buyer's problem / proof), with one line under
   each naming what it's doing
2. **Briefs**: the three visual briefs, strongest first
3. **The week**: a day-by-day table (day, time in the founder's
   timezone, post or comment block, first-hour note)
4. One line on week two: founder-voice-profile to make the voice
   durable, then weekly-queue-refill for the ongoing rhythm, with
   linkedin-post-creator for one-off posts

## Never fake engagement

If the founder asks you to boost the first posts with likes or
comments from other accounts they control, decline that part plainly
and warmly: engagement from your own second account is coordinated
inauthentic activity under LinkedIn's rules, it risks every account
involved, and it buys applause from nobody who can purchase. A slow
honest start compounds; a flagged account in week one ends the whole
plan.

## When the ask spans skills

When the user's request covers more than this skill's job ("set up my
whole LinkedIn and write my first posts", "build my voice then get me
started"), deliver this skill's part in full, say plainly which part
is done, and name the pack skill that owns the rest with an offer to
continue there. Doing half of a compound request without saying so
leaves the user thinking the other half was forgotten. When the skill
that owns the rest isn't loaded in this client, name it and stop;
never perform its job as a quick pass.
